Panel

Students use AI during exams in a chat window beside the test. Professors watch how they work with it - and grade that, not just the answer.

AI is already in the room during every exam. Banning it produces students who hide what they're doing; allowing it without any structure produces students who outsource their thinking entirely. Panel is a third path: let students use AI, watch how they use it, and teach them the difference between relying on it and leveraging it.

The distinction matters. A student who asks the AI "what is Porter's Five Forces?" and copies the answer is outsourcing. A student who says "I think the strongest force here is rivalry because of Ryanair's cost structure - does that reasoning hold?" is using AI to pressure-test their own thinking. Panel surfaces that difference clearly so professors can grade it and students can see it in themselves.

The premise

The question is not whether students use AI - they do, and they will. The question is whether schools teach them to use it well. The skill that matters is leverage: bringing your own knowledge and judgment to a conversation with AI, rather than delegating your thinking to it.

Panel makes that skill visible and gradable. By letting professors observe the full chat transcript - not just the submitted answer - they can assess how a student reasons under pressure, not just what answer they produced. A strong AI-assisted answer reached through poor reasoning is a different outcome than a strong answer reached through sharp, AI-enhanced thinking.

How it works

  1. The professor enables Panel for a specific exam through a Canvas LMS integration. Students get a standard exam link - the Panel chat window opens automatically beside the test questions.
  2. During the exam, students can send messages to an AI assistant in the right-hand panel. The full conversation is logged in real time and captured at submission.
  3. After submission, the professor sees a per-student analytics view: an AI reliance vs. leverage score, a breakdown of how often the student prompted AI vs. extended their own reasoning, and the full captured transcript.
  4. The professor uses that data alongside the submitted answers to grade both the quality of thinking and how well the student leveraged AI as a tool rather than a substitute for thought.

What the score measures

The reliance vs. leverage score is built from the conversation structure. High-reliance sessions look like a series of short, closed questions with the student accepting AI output and moving on. High-leverage sessions show a student who pushes back, adds context, tests their own claims against AI responses, and synthesizes a position that is clearly their own.

The score is not a cheating detector. Panel assumes students are allowed to use AI. The score is a measure of intellectual engagement - the same thing a professor tries to assess from an answer alone, but with much richer signal.

The beta

The beta is a static mockup of both views: the exam screen a student sees during a test, and the analytics dashboard a professor sees after submissions close.

  • Exam screen - the question area and AI chat side-panel, showing an example student session.
  • Professor analytics - per-student scores, reliance vs. leverage breakdowns, and a captured transcript sample.
