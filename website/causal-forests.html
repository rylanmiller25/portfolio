Causal Forests

An experiment - like an A/B test - tells you what a change does on average. But the average hides who it actually worked for: some people were won over, some were always going to buy, and some didn't care at all.

Causal forests pull those groups apart. Instead of one overall number, they estimate how much each individual person responds - so you can aim an offer at the people it actually moves, and stop wasting it on everyone else.

This project builds that tool and shows it works on a simulated discount experiment where the true answer is known ahead of time.

A standard experiment estimates the Average Treatment Effect (ATE) - the mean outcome difference between treated and control. It answers "does it work?" but collapses all variation into a single number.

Causal forests estimate Conditional Average Treatment Effects (CATEs) - the effect as a function of covariates, τ(x) = E[Y(1) − Y(0) | X = x]. This build uses generalized random forests (GRF) with honest trees (2,000 trees, honesty fraction 0.5): one subsample chooses the splits and a disjoint subsample estimates effects within each leaf, which de-biases the estimates.

It runs in simulation mode - a synthetic seat-discount RCT of 10,000 enterprise customers with known, heterogeneous true CATEs (varying by geography, company size, pricing tier, and tenure) - so every estimate is checkable against ground truth. The pipeline runs the full GRF diagnostic suite: overlap, IPW covariate balance, the calibration test, and RATE/AUTOC for value-of-targeting.

Results: mean calibration ≈ 1.0 (well-calibrated ATE), significant differential prediction (genuine heterogeneity, with the expected forest shrinkage), and CATE-by-covariate breakdowns that recover the simulated structure.
